**Role:** PI · **Funder:** National Natural Science Foundation of China (NSFC), Young Scientist Project · **Award:** ~$41,000 USD · **Period:** 2025 – 2027

Valley bank gullies sit at the junction of hillslopes and valley slopes on the Chinese Loess Plateau. They reshape the terrain and route runoff arriving from both above and below, so they change the flow characteristics — and therefore the erosion behaviour — of the valley slopes beneath them. Sitting in the middle and lower part of the hillslope–valley-slope system, and receiving upstream runoff onto their own steep faces, they are highly prone to landslides and collapses.

The motivation is a shift in where erosion happens. As vegetation restoration across the Loess Plateau has suppressed hillslope erosion, **valley bank gullies have increasingly become the erosion hotspots** — yet their effect on valley slope erosion, and the mechanisms behind it, remain little studied, which limits effective control.

The project surveys three valley slope types with differing degrees of gully influence in the Fangta and Houyaoxian watersheds (Ansai, Yan'an, Shaanxi), using **UAV photogrammetry** for high-precision imagery alongside environmental field surveys, in order to: characterise the distribution, scale and morphology of valley bank gullies and their effect on valley slope terrain and flow; extract the form, location and intensity of valley slope erosion and analyse the mechanisms involved; and classify all gullies in the Fangta watershed to rank their relative contributions and identify which valley slope types to prioritise for control.
