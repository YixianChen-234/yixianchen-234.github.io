 Curriculum Vitae

I study how water reshapes land, and how to make that predictable at scale. At the Surface Dynamics Modeling Lab I work on flood inundation mapping (FIM) for CIROH, a NOAA cooperative institute: with a spatial joining framework I built, I carried satellite river slopes from NASA’s ICESat-2 and SWOT missions onto the river network of NOAA’s operational HAND-FIM system, where they improved flood-map accuracy by an average of 31% across eight U.S. flood events and remain in operational use. I am now PI on a $300,000 NOAA CIROH project on how best to calibrate the model’s synthetic rating curves. My longer-running work is on gully erosion, from nearly a decade of fieldwork on the Chinese Loess Plateau, where I lead a National Natural Science Foundation of China project, to the first global assessment of gully occurrence, whose maps are archived by the European Commission’s Joint Research Centre and were covered by the BBC.
