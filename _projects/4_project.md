On 26 July 2017 an extreme rainstorm with an estimated 200-year return period struck Zizhou County in northern Shaanxi. I went to study gully erosion, and measured everything else as well — rill erosion, landsliding, and the failure of the terraces and check dams built to control erosion in the first place.

That decision produced the finding. Across three catchments:

| Process | Intensity (t km⁻²) | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Landsliding | 4,433 ± 657 | 37 ± 4% |
| **Terrace & check-dam collapse** | **3,927 ± 1,009** | **33 ± 4%** |
| Rill erosion | 2,815 ± 529 | 24 ± 3% |
| Gully erosion | 787 ± 486 | 7 ± 4% |

The engineered erosion controls, once they failed, became the **second-largest sediment source in the catchment**. Many of those failures were traceable to maintenance gaps and design choices rather than to the storm alone — which points to concrete remedies: protect terrace embankments, provide runoff evacuation, size check-dam flood-release facilities properly, and prefer many small check dams over a few large ones.

The low contribution from gully erosion is the mirror-image result: it is evidence that decades of vegetation restoration under the Grain-for-Green programme have worked.
