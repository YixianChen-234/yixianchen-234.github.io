Research Highlights

Selected work on flood inundation mapping and soil erosion.

Flood

Improved river slope for the U.S. operational flood model

Chen Y, Cohen S, Baruah A, Devi D, Dhital S, Tian D, Munasinghe D (2025). Scientific Data 12, 1657. DOI · data
River slope drives the synthetic rating curves inside NOAA's operational HAND-FIM system, but those slopes came from a hydroconditioned national DEM that cannot resolve the near-flat gradients of real rivers — and, being static, could not track change. I replaced them with slopes from NASA's ICESat-2 laser altimetry (IRIS), supplemented by SWORD.

The improved slopes raised flood-map accuracy by an average of 31% (Critical Success Index) across eight U.S. flood events, and were merged into NOAA-OWP/inundation-mapping in May 2025, where the operational system still uses them.

riverjoin — making satellite data reach operational models

Open-source Python package. GitHub · PyPI
Hydrographic datasets disagree on flowline density, length, spatial offset and network topology, and that is precisely why better satellite measurements could not be attached to the operational river network. riverjoin reconciles them: it joins any two linear river networks given only geometry and basic connectivity fields, and transfers attributes in both directions.

Demonstrated across six dataset pairs — HAND-FIM ↔ SWORD, IRIS and GEOGLOWS; SWORD ↔ GEOGLOWS, MERIT Basins and GRIT — from catchment to global scale. It also powers GEOGLOWS-driven flood mapping in FIMserv, extending operational-grade flood inundation mapping beyond the United States.

Temporal variability of global river slope from SWOT

Chen Y, Cohen S, Dhital S, Devi D, Baruah A (2026). Geophysical Research Letters, under revision. data
Operational models treat river slope as a fixed property, but water-surface slope varies with discharge, season and channel change. Using Hydrocron, NASA's SWOT time-series service, I collected every available observation worldwide between August 2023 and May 2025, queried against SWORD v16 reach identifiers.

For each reach I extracted water-surface slope, elevation, width and detected area together with their uncertainty estimates and quality flags — producing a global, monthly, reach-level slope dataset that carries its own error budget, and the basis for supplying time-corresponding rather than static slope to flood models.

Optimal synthetic rating curve calibration

NOAA CIROH FY26 · PI · $300K total · 2026–2027
NOAA's operational HAND-FIM system turns National Water Model flow forecasts into flood maps through a synthetic rating curve for every river reach — a computed link between flow and water level, built from the channel's shape, slope and roughness and calibrated against observed floods. Better versions of those inputs now exist, but improving one in isolation can make the calibrated model worse, because the calibration was balanced around the old values — something I found while validating the river slope work. This project tests eleven calibration scenarios combining improved riverbed shape (bathymetry), slope and land-cover-based roughness, and trains models that recommend the best calibration for each reach, evaluated over roughly 1,000 reach-scale benchmark samples.

First results from three benchmark floods show that the answer depends on the place and the event — no single calibration wins everywhere. In Conway, Arkansas, land-cover-based roughness raised map accuracy by 18% and halved false alarms compared with the current operational calibration; in Goldsboro, North Carolina, improved bathymetry gained 11% even though the operational model already did well there. Stacking every improvement at once raised accuracy but inflated false alarms — the trade-off that makes a per-reach recommendation necessary.

Soil Erosion

Can gullies be trusted from satellite imagery?

Chen Y, Jiao J, Wei Y, Zhao H, Yu W, Cao B (2019). International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health 16(3), 369.
Most gully studies interpret morphology from high-resolution imagery without first testing whether the imagery represents gullies accurately. Before mapping gullies at regional scale, I measured how well it works.

Using Pleiades-1B imagery at 0.5 m panchromatic resolution, gully planform can be interpreted accurately overall: larger gullies are captured more accurately, more complex planforms less so, and winter imagery is largely unaffected by vegetation. From field-measured gully area and perimeter I built correction models — R² = 0.99 for area and R² = 0.98 for perimeter (n = 130) — which made the regional work that followed defensible.

Gully morphology and density along a rainfall gradient

Chen Y, Jiao J, Yan X, Li J, Vanmaercke M, Wang N (2024). Catena 236, 107773.
Gully research on the Chinese Loess Plateau had concentrated on individual gullies and small watersheds. This study asked how gully morphology and density vary across the whole region, and what controls that variation.

Gully dimensions increase from north to south — width most of all — under the joint influence of rainfall and the slope of the contributing drainage area. Number, length and area densities all decrease as vegetation cover rises, and number density matters more than gully size in setting overall density. Rainy-season rainfall and vegetation cover emerge as the dominant regional controls, with rainfall's indirect effect through vegetation outweighing its direct effect on gully occurrence.

Gully development in response to upslope land cover, 1976–2019

Chen Y (2021). Morphological Spatial Variation and Development Mechanisms of Gullies on the Chinese Loess Plateau. PhD dissertation, University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, Chapter 4. In Chinese with English abstract. The 1976–1997 results are published in Chen et al. (2017).
Whether the Grain-for-Green programme slowed gully erosion can only be judged against what gullies did before it. In the Fangta catchment (Ansai, Loess Plateau) I reconstructed the valley edges of seven large gullies from 1:10,000 topographic maps for 1976 and 1997 and from Google Earth imagery for 2008 and 2017, monitored small-gully heads in the field with RTK GNSS and erosion pins over 2016–2019, and related each period's gully development to land use and vegetation cover in the upslope drainage area.

As cropland gave way to terraces, forest and grassland, gully erosion fell sharply. The density of actively retreating small-gully heads on the large gullies dropped from 4,099 ± 2,031 to 1,140 ± 187 per km², the annual growth of valley area from 3.21 ± 2.76% to 1.48 ± 0.90%, and head retreat from 0.49 to 0.30 to 0.011 m yr−1 across 1976–1997, 2008–2017 and 2016–2019. Valley widening, headward erosion and new small gullies all drove growth before the programme; by 2008–2017 only a much-reduced widening remained. Terraces curb widening and new gullies by cutting runoff, while head retreat responds most to vegetation, especially grass, which becomes increasingly effective once vegetation cover exceeds about 40–45%.

Catchment-scale erosion contributions of different processes

Chen Y, Vanmaercke M, Jiao J, Bai L, Tang B, Wang N, Zhang Y, Wang H (2023). Land Degradation & Development 34(2), 403–422. DOI
After a rainstorm with an estimated 200-year return period struck Zizhou County in July 2017, I went to study gully erosion — and measured every other process as well. That decision produced the result.

Landsliding contributed 4,433 ± 657 t km−2 (37 ± 4%) and the collapse of terraces and check dams 3,927 ± 1,009 t km−2 (33 ± 4%), ahead of rill erosion (24 ± 3%) and gully erosion (7 ± 4%). The engineered erosion controls, once they failed, were the second-largest sediment source in the catchment — many failures traceable to maintenance gaps and design choices rather than to the storm alone. The low gully contribution is the mirror-image finding: evidence that vegetation restoration has worked.

The first global map of gully occurrence

Chen Y, De Geeter S, Poesen J, Matthews F, Campforts B, Borrelli P, Panagos P, Vanmaercke M (2026). International Soil and Water Conservation Research 14, 100572. DOI · ESDAC
Gully erosion drives land degradation worldwide, but no global picture existed of where gullies occur or what controls them. The bottleneck was labelled data, not algorithms.

I designed a rapid scoring scheme — each 1 km² site scored from high-resolution satellite imagery in one to two minutes — and generated a global training set of 17,499 sites, with repeat interpretation agreeing at r² = 0.95. A 100-model random forest ensemble over 26 environmental predictors then mapped gully head density and susceptibility at 1 km² resolution, published with an explicit uncertainty layer.

Anthropogenically sensitive hotspots include the Chinese Loess Plateau, the Ethiopian Highlands, and much of the Mediterranean and Sahel. Deserts also show high gully densities, but there the cause is natural — a distinction with direct consequences for land management.
